Fermentation // Herbal Dyes

Dye Chemistry: Plant Fermentations & Color Fixers

Chemical dye fixers can leave harsh residues on bedding textiles. Fermenting herbal dyes in plant-extracted vats delivers natural colors. This guide details herbal dye chemistry and organic color fixing methods.

The Physics of Herbal Dye Fermentation

Herbal leaves are fermented in warm water, letting bacteria extract the natural dye pigments. The vat requires constant temperature control.

We add organic sugars to feed the bacteria, maintaining an active fermentation vat that reduces dye pigments into soluble color molecules.

Let us also consider the role of natural yeast starters. Mixing yeast into the herbal dye vat speeds up bacterial reduction, converting the pigments quickly without requiring chemical starters.

Natural Mordants and Tannin Baths

Linen fibers lack natural affinity for plant dyes. We soak the raw fabric in tea leaf tannin baths before dyeing.

The tannin acts as a natural mordant, binding the dye pigments to the flax cells, securing color fastness under light wash cycles.

Additionally, check the dye vat temperature details. Settle water conditions early to avoid vat deaths, and check the pH levels to ensure they stay in the alkaline range, protecting natural color bonds.

Flexible rubber remains elastic in cold heat, providing grip and comfort. However, traditional artisans utilize hand-turned molds that compress the rubber, allowing the outsole to develop a rugged trend pattern.

Managing Vat Fermentation pH Levels

We measure vat pH levels using digital meters, keeping the fermentation active. Settle alkaline agent values early to avoid vat deaths.

An active vat has a clear, yellow-green sheen, indicating reduced pigments. We monitor this state daily to secure dye quality.

When dipping the linen sheets, stir the vat slowly. Stirring gently helps you mix the dye pigments evenly, reducing streaks, and coordinates with your dipping routine, helping you get clean colors.

Testing Color Fading on Sun Simulator Lamps

We check color fastness under sun simulator lamps, recording fade rates. Record dye lots, vat pH, exposure hours, and fade ratings in a log.

Comparing these metrics allows us to refine our herbal dye formulas, ensuring our dyed sheets hold their rich colors.
